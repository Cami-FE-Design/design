# PRO-737, CamiPay fee visibility on the Partner side

Companion to [PRO-737, Cami HQ: CamiPay settlement config](./PRO-737-cami-hq-camipay-config.md),
which covers the HQ half. That spec is where the rate model, the append-only
rate card, and the forward-only rule are defined. This one covers what the
**Partner** sees of it.

Surface: the **Business app**. Two places, no new routes.

Status: **UI shipped**, mock data only, no backend.

Implementation:

- `components/blocks/camipay-fee-breakdown.tsx`, the per-sale breakdown
- `components/blocks/payment-policy/camipay-rates-panel.tsx`, the settings view
- `app/sales/sales-list/page.tsx`, hosts the breakdown in the sale detail
- `lib/hq-camipay/store.tsx`, `computeFee` / `explainFee`

## Problem

HQ can now set a rate. The Partner has no way to see what it cost them.

Two consequences, both of which end in a support ticket:

1. **A payment settles for less than the sale total and nothing explains it.**
   The Partner reconciles their own books against Cami's payouts and finds a gap
   they cannot account for.
2. **The rate itself is invisible.** A Partner who agreed a rate has nowhere to
   confirm it, so the only record of a commercial term is a conversation.

Maaz, asked whether the fee is config-only until settlement is built: *"definitely
a). We need to calculate EVERY payment transaction from now."* The fee is
computed and shown per payment from day one, not deferred to the settlement
engine.

## Scope

Two surfaces.

1. **Fee breakdown on the sale detail**, for sales paid over CamiPay.
2. **A read-only rate view** at Settings → Payments → CamiPay rates.

Out of scope, flagged deliberately:

- **Payout and settlement statements.** A per-payment breakdown is not a
  statement. Batch payouts, settlement periods, and the reconciliation view are
  a separate surface, and the account-statement export Maaz shared belongs
  there rather than here.
- **Refund fee treatment.** Whether Cami returns its fee on a refund is a
  commercial decision nobody has made. `computeFee` takes an absolute amount and
  says nothing about reversal; the breakdown is not rendered on a refund line.
- **Fee totals in reports.** The reporting registry has no fee column yet.
- **Anything the Partner can change.** Rates are set by Cami.

## The two rules that shape both surfaces

### Cami's fee only, never the gateway's

Maaz, verbatim: *"This is the Cami Platform commission charged to the
Merchant/Business. The Merchant/Business will ONLY see this fee, not the
processing fees of the payment gateway providers (NeoPay/NI/Stripe etc)."*

So neither surface names a gateway or shows a processing cost. What Cami pays
NeoPay or Tap sits behind the single number the Partner sees. Both surfaces say
so positively rather than leaving the absence to be noticed:

> This is everything Cami charges on this payment.

The sentence is doing real work. Without it, a Partner who knows card processing
exists assumes a second deduction is coming.

### The rate is snapshotted, not looked up

`Sale.camipay` carries a `rate` copied from the rate card at capture. The
breakdown renders that stored rate, never `effectiveRate(...)` resolved now. A
renegotiation must not restate a sale that already happened (INV-01), and the
only way to guarantee that is for the past sale to stop depending on the card.

The mock sales spell their snapshots out as constants for the same reason: it
models what a capture actually does.

## Fee breakdown on the sale detail

Sits inside the sale card, directly under the Payment line, divided from it.
Rendered only when the sale has a `camipay` snapshot, is not voided, and money
actually moved. A fee on a voided or unpaid sale is a number for a thing that
never happened.

GNK's requested sequence, followed exactly:

| Row | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Sale amount | What was captured, `AED 30.40` |
| Cami fee | `- AED 1.66`, with `3% of AED 30.40 + AED 0.75` underneath |
| Net to you | `AED 28.74`, emphasised, above a divider |

The sub-line under the fee is the requirement "make it clear how the fee was
calculated". It is generated by `explainFee`, so it always describes the
arithmetic that actually ran, including dropping the fixed term when the bracket
excluded it.

**The base is what was captured, not the sale total and not what was tendered.**
The sale detail's `collectedMinor` (paid, capped at the sale total) is what gets
passed. A completed sale's paid figure includes change handed back, and a
part-paid sale has collected less than its total. Charging on either of the
wrong two numbers is a real money bug, so the choice is commented at the call
site.

**No rail heading.** The payment line above already carries the CamiPay Terminal
or Online pill. Repeating it read as a second payment rather than a breakdown of
the first.

**Zero rate.** When the snapshot is `ZERO_RATE` the whole table is replaced by
one line: "No Cami fee applies to this CamiPay Terminal payment. The full
AED 42.00 settles to you." A fee row reading `AED 0.00` looks like a rounding
artefact; a sentence reads like a term.

**Footnote**, always present:

> Charged at your rate on 25 May 2026, and locked to it. This is everything Cami
> charges on this payment.

The date is the snapshot made visible. It is what lets a Partner who has just
renegotiated understand why an old sale still shows the old number.

## Settings → Payments → CamiPay rates

A fourth card on the Payments settings landing, alongside Payment policy,
Payment methods, and Terminals. Deep link `?settings=payments&pp=camipay`.

Maaz: *"merchant's can only see the final rate set in their CamiPay business
settings."*

One card per rail:

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Icon tile | Violet when the rail is live, muted when not, matching the HQ rails card |
| Label | `CamiPay Terminal` / `CamiPay Online`, with a muted `Not enabled` badge when off |
| Rate | `1.8%` or `3% + AED 0.75`, or `No fee` |
| Bracket | `AED 0.75 applies under AED 100.00, 3% alone at or above` |
| Provenance | `In place since 01 May 2026` |
| Upcoming | An amber strip for any future-dated row: `1.9% from 01 Sep 2026` |

**Read-only in the strong sense.** No disabled fields, no greyed buttons, no
edit affordance of any kind. A disabled control implies a permission the Partner
could be granted; what they actually need is their account manager, which the
footnote tells them.

**Scheduled rates are shown.** A Partner finding out about an agreed rate change
from an invoice is how trust in the number goes. HQ badges these `Scheduled`;
the Partner-facing word is `Upcoming`, because the Partner did not schedule
anything.

**Footnote:**

> This is everything Cami charges. Card processing is already covered by it, and
> nothing else is deducted from a payment. Each payment keeps the rate that was
> live on the day it was taken, so a rate change never affects money you have
> already been paid. To discuss your rates, talk to your Cami account manager.

## Wiring

The panel reads `useCamiPay()` against `DEMO_MERCHANT_ID` (`biz_shampooch`), the
Partner the Business app stands in for. The store is mounted in the HQ layout,
so the panel wraps itself in `CamiPayProvider` rather than the app root taking
on HQ state it has no other use for. Both providers hydrate from the same
`cami-hq-camipay-v2` localStorage key, so a rate changed in HQ shows up here,
which is the point when demoing the two together.

## Known gaps

- **Refunds show no fee line.** Deliberate, see Scope, but it means a refunded
  sale's detail is silent about what happened to the original fee.
- **Tips are not separated.** The fee is charged on the captured amount as one
  number. Whether a tip should carry the platform fee is unanswered.
- **The settings panel is Business-level.** Same inheritance declaration as the
  HQ rate card (INV-10), and it will need a per-location answer at the same time
  HQ does.
- **No fee history.** The Partner can see today's rate and any upcoming change,
  but not what they used to pay. HQ has the full history; whether the Partner
  should is a commercial call.
- **The account-statement export is not built.** Maaz attached one to the thread
  as the target for Sales/Invoice detail. The per-sale breakdown covers the
  detail screen; the statement itself needs the settlement surface.
