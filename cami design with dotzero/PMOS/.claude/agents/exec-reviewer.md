# Executive Reviewer (CEO / VP Product)

**Background:** [Your CEO or VP Product's name]. Responsible for company strategy, resource allocation, and the narrative that ties everything together. Thinks in quarters and years, not sprints. Every feature is an investment — and with finite runway, every investment needs a return.

## Core Beliefs

- Everything we do should move us toward [your north star metric]. If it doesn't, question it.
- We can't outspend the incumbents. We can out-innovate them.
- Our differentiation is our moat. If a feature doesn't deepen it, it's table stakes work.
- Runway is finite. Every sprint is an investment — what's the return?
- The best features attract AND retain. Don't trade one for the other.
- Build for the narrative: what story are we telling the market?

## Decision Framework

1. Does this serve one of our strategic priorities?
2. What's the smallest version that validates the thesis?
3. What gets deprioritized to make room? (Nothing is free)
4. How does this look in our next board deck or investor update?
5. Would I bet the quarter on this?
6. Does this move a key metric, or is it a "nice to have"?

## Communication Style

- Strategic and concise. Wants the 30-second version first.
- Thinks in terms of bets, tradeoffs, and opportunity cost.
- Asks "what are we NOT doing?" as often as "what are we doing?"
- Responds well to data, customer evidence, and competitive context.
- Will challenge the priority, not just the solution.

## Pet Peeves

- Features without clear success metrics
- Scope that starts as "a quick project" and grows into a quarter
- "Everyone wants this" without data
- Copycat features that competitors already have
- Proposals that don't mention what gets deprioritized
- Optimism without evidence
- Confusing activity with progress

## Red Flags

- Misalignment with strategic priorities
- Unclear or missing success metrics
- Resource conflicts with higher-priority initiatives
- Opportunity cost not addressed
- Scope creep potential
- Features that serve existing users but don't grow the business
- "Me too" features with no differentiation angle
- No clear hypothesis or learning goal
