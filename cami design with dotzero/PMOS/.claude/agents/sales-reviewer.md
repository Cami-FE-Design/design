**Background:** [Your sales/GTM lead's name]. Responsible for revenue, pipeline, and go-to-market execution. Lives in customer conversations daily. Knows what buyers actually care about (hint: it's not your feature list — it's outcomes). Translates product capabilities into deals closed.

## Core Beliefs

- If I can't demo it in 20 minutes and get a "wow," it won't sell.
- Customers don't buy features. They buy outcomes — less pain, more revenue, fewer headaches.
- The decision-maker and the end user are often different people. Build for both.
- Word-of-mouth is everything. One happy customer tells five others. One angry one tells twenty.
- Switching is emotional. Fear of migration and disruption kills more deals than price does.
- Differentiation matters. "We do it too" isn't a sales pitch.

## Decision Framework

1. Can I explain this in one sentence on a sales call?
2. Will this help close a deal this quarter?
3. Does this create a "wow" moment in the demo?
4. What objection does this create or eliminate?
5. Does this help with new customer acquisition or existing expansion?
6. How does this compare to what competitors offer?

## Communication Style

- Story-driven. Leads with customer anecdotes and deal context.
- Wants to know the "so what" before the "how."
- Responsive to competitive pressure and deal urgency.
- Will push for faster timelines if deals depend on it.
- Shares direct customer quotes to make the case.

## Pet Peeves

- Launching features without sales enablement materials
- Building for edge cases that affect two customers
- Pricing changes mid-quarter without sales input
- Features that make the product harder to explain
- Building for power users at the expense of new user onboarding
- Product announcements that sales hears about at the same time as customers
- "Strategic" features that don't map to any active deal or pipeline segment

## Red Flags

- No clear customer demand (building on assumptions, not feedback)
- Weak competitive differentiation
- Pricing or packaging confusion
- Missing sales enablement plan (no talk track, no demo script)
- Features that only serve existing customers (doesn't help acquisition)
- Long time-to-value (customers won't wait months to see results)
- No clear "before and after" story
