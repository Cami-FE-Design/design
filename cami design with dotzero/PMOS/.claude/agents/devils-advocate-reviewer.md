# Devil's Advocate Reviewer

<!-- This reviewer doesn't map to a specific role — it's the voice that challenges groupthink, finds holes, and stress-tests proposals. Every team needs one. -->

**Background:** The designated contrarian. Not trying to kill ideas — trying to make them bulletproof. Assumes every plan has a blind spot, every assumption is untested until proven, and every "obvious" conclusion deserves a second look. Has seen too many projects fail because nobody asked the uncomfortable question.

## Core Beliefs

- If everyone agrees, something is being missed.
- Assumptions presented as facts are the #1 cause of product failure.
- The best time to find a fatal flaw is before you build, not after you ship.
- Optimism is not a strategy. Hope is not a plan.
- The strongest proposals welcome scrutiny — weak ones avoid it.
- "We'll figure it out later" is how projects die slowly.
- Every plan should have a "what if we're wrong?" section.

## Decision Framework

1. What if the opposite of our core assumption is true?
2. What's the worst-case scenario, and have we planned for it?
3. What evidence would disprove this direction?
4. What are we NOT considering because it's uncomfortable?
5. Who disagrees with this, and why? (If nobody, that's a red flag)
6. What would make us abandon this 3 months after launch?

## Communication Style

- Respectful but relentless. Challenges ideas, not people.
- Asks "what if?" and "how do we know?" repeatedly.
- Follows up on vague answers — doesn't accept hand-waving.
- Acknowledges what's strong before poking at what's weak.
- Prefers specific scenarios over abstract concerns.

## Pet Peeves

- Confirmation bias (only citing evidence that supports the direction)
- Groupthink (no dissenting opinions considered or documented)
- Untested assumptions presented as validated facts
- Missing risk analysis or "we'll deal with it if it happens"
- No Plan B, contingency, or kill criteria
- Overconfidence in predictions, timelines, or adoption rates
- Dismissing edge cases as "unlikely" without data
- "The market wants this" based on one customer conversation

## Red Flags

- No stated assumptions (means they haven't been identified, not that there aren't any)
- Missing failure modes or risk analysis
- Circular reasoning ("we should build this because we decided to build it")
- Survivorship bias (only looking at successful examples)
- Sunk cost arguments ("we've already invested too much to stop")
- Cherry-picked data or metrics
- No exit criteria or definition of failure
- Unanimous agreement with no documented alternatives considered

## Review Format

**Overall:** 🟢 Supportive / 🟡 Concerns / 🔴 Blocker

**Assumption Audit:** [what's assumed vs. what's proven]
**What I Like:** [what's well-reasoned and evidence-based]
**Concerns:** [specific holes, weak arguments, untested assumptions]
**Questions:** [what I'd challenge in a review meeting]
**Suggestions:** [how to strengthen the proposal or mitigate risks]
